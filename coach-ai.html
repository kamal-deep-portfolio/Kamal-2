01 / Context

Prepare for the conversation. Then rehearse it.

A manager may need help deciding how to approach a performance concern, or want to rehearse the conversation before having it.

Coach AI brings both activities into one experience: ask for coaching guidance or practice a scenario with a simulated associate.

I owned the experience design, working with the product owner to align the interface with the coaching use cases. Team managers are the direct audience; the coaching program supports a population of 8,000 Customer Service associates.

02 / Actual product

Work through a real concern.

The conversational experience lets managers ask about situations such as an associate prioritizing speed at the expense of customer-interaction quality.

The interface separates the manager’s question from the AI’s response and keeps a follow-up input available beneath the exchange. In the product example, Coach AI organizes guidance around the Situation–Behavior–Impact framework, followed by suggested ways to collaborate on improvement and provide support.

03 / Interaction design

Give the practice a structure.

The scenario-practice designs let managers choose both a situation and an associate response style. The interface explains the roles: the manager conducts the coaching conversation while the AI plays the associate.

Scenario context remains available alongside the exchange. Custom inputs let managers describe situations beyond the predefined options.

The designed flow includes an explicit action to end practice and request AI-generated coaching feedback.

The practice visuals show the designed experience. An actual post-practice feedback screen is not included in the supplied evidence.

04 / Expectations

Make the limitations visible.

The actual product screenshot places two important expectations near the conversation:

  • AI can make mistakes, and important information should be checked.
  • Chat history is not saved in the represented version.

These messages give managers context for using the guidance and deciding what information to retain. They describe the interface’s expectations, not a technical guarantee about safeguards or data handling.
