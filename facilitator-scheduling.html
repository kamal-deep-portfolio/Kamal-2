01 / Context

The work behind every session

Leadership-learning programs depended on facilitators finding suitable sessions and committing their time. Program managers needed to ensure those sessions had coverage.

The existing process spread this work across sign-up documents, email requests, and manual calendar coordination. Facilitators certified for multiple programs could receive several different requests in a week.

I led the UX and UI design of a shared experience for finding sessions, managing commitments, and identifying gaps in coverage.

02 / Structure

Two audiences, connected responsibilities.

For facilitators

Find suitable opportunities and keep track of sessions they have agreed to teach.

For program managers

See coverage across scheduled sessions, including primary and backup assignments.

The design provided distinct views for these responsibilities while connecting them through the same session information. I worked with technical partners to align the proposed interactions with implementation constraints.

03 / Evidence → Decision

The information was there. The urgency wasn’t.

In facilitator usability testing, 8 of 9 participants did not recognize that “Seeking facilitators” communicated an urgent staffing need.

Original wording

Seeking facilitators

↓
Revised wording

Urgent Facilitation Requests

Programs needing facilitators within the next two weeks.

I revised the heading and gave the explanation greater visual prominence. The change made the reason to act explicit, rather than expecting people to infer urgency from a list of sessions.

Wording comparison from the case-study record, not a screenshot of the original interface.

04 / Interaction

Make the next action easier to find.

4 of 9 facilitator participants struggled to locate the sign-up entry point. The revised design used the explicit label “Session Sign-up” and strengthened its visibility.

Within the scheduling view, calendar and program filters helped users narrow the available sessions. The upcoming-session area separately identified primary and backup responsibilities and exposed an option to resend an invitation.

05 / Delivery

Less coordination behind the learning.

The platform launched in October 2023. The redesigned workflow helped reduce weekly program-coordination time from 18 hours to 8 hours.

This was a product-level result delivered with program and technical partners. My contribution was to translate the coordination problem into a shared interface, test where it confused users, and refine the design around those findings.

Working within release constraints

Testing also identified admin-side issues, including difficulty locating cancellation. Several recommended improvements were deferred to a later release.

The supplied visuals are design artifacts. Final release scope and cancellation wording are being reconciled before publication; these visuals do not establish which changes shipped.
