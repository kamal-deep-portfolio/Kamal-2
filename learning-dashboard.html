01 / Context

Bring a fragmented learning picture together.

Leadership development involved several kinds of activity: learning paths, individual programs, practical applications, and toolkits.

The dashboard needed to bring these together while helping leaders distinguish what they had completed, what was still in progress, and what they could explore next.

I worked with program and technical partners to define the experience, tested the designs, and implemented the front end.

02 / Interaction

Connect progress to a next step.

The design combined an overview of learning activity with three views: In Progress, Completed, and Recommendations.

In-progress items provided the learning-path context, activity title, estimated duration, and a direct Resume action. That connected the progress overview to something the learner could do.

03 / Content design

Make optional learning feel optional.

The recommendation section introduced development opportunities tailored to the learner’s job level.

Supporting text explicitly described these suggestions as optional, rather than mandatory parts of a learning path. This distinction communicated the different purpose of recommendations alongside existing commitments.

I collaborated with a learning technologist on the initial recommendation framework.

04 / Design → Implementation

Responsibility beyond the mockup.

My responsibility extended beyond producing a prototype. I built the front end using React and Bootstrap, working on responsive behavior and consistency with the existing platform.

The implementation used modular components. A working prototype also gave stakeholders a concrete experience to review ahead of launch.

The visual shown here is an earlier design reconstructed from Figma. A current implementation capture is still needed; it is not presented as a production screenshot or a live product demo.

05 / Validation

Test the experience, not just the layout.

I conducted usability testing for the dashboard and personalized recommendations, documented the findings, and used them to inform iterations.
